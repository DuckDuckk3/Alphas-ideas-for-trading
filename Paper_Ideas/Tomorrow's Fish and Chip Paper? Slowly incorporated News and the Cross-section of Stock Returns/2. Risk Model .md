## Explaination  
To prove the strategy's validity, the authors test the returns against five increasingly rigorous models. The goal is to see if the Alpha ($\alpha$) remains statistically significant.
- FF3F (3-Factor): Market risk, Size (SMB), and Value (HML).
- FF4F (Carhart 4-Factor): Adds Momentum (WML).
- FF5F (5-Factor): Adds Profitability (RMW) and Investment (CMA).
- FF4F + Liq: Adds Liquidity risk (to see if the returns only come from "hard-to-trade" stocks).
- FF4F + Rev: Adds Short-term Reversal (to see if the strategy is just a "bounce back" from yesterday's move).

The Regression Formula:  
$$R_{it} - R_{ft} = \alpha_i + \beta_{i1}(R_{mt} - R_{ft}) + \beta_{i2}SMB_t + \beta_{i3}HML_t + ... + \epsilon_{it}$$

If $\alpha$ is significantly greater than zero, the news strategy is providing returns that these standard factors cannot explain.  

## Key concepts  
### SI vs. QI News
The study compares SI (Specific Information) and QI (Quantifiable Information).
- `SI (Specific Information)`: High-quality, unique news content. Its Alpha remains significant across all models, proving it is a pure alpha source.
- `QI (Quantifiable Information)`: Standard, data-heavy news. Its Alpha disappears in the 3-factor and 5-factor models, indicating it is redundant (it simply mimics existing factors such as Size or Value).
- `SMQ (SI Minus QI)`: A long-short portfolio that confirms SI news is a significantly stronger predictor than QI news.

| News Type | Performance Across Models | Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **SI News** (Specific Info) | **Highly Significant Alpha** | The information is unique. It is not a proxy for liquidity or short-term price reversals. |
| **QI News** (Quantifiable Info) | **Insignificant Alpha** | Gains disappear after controlling for FF3F/FF5F. QI news merely captures existing factors. |
| **SMQ Portfolio** (SI - QI) | **Significant** | Confirms that SI news has much stronger predictive power compared to QI news. |

### Debunking the "Reversal" Myth
A common critique of news-based strategies is that they just capture Short-term Reversal (stocks that dropped too far on news and "bounced").
- Evidence: Even when the Rev factor was added to the regression, the SI news alpha stayed significant.
- Conclusion: The "SI news effect" is a distinct phenomenon. It represents a genuine reaction to information, not just a mechanical price correction.

### Derived Insights
* **The Reversal Myth**: The SI news effect remains significant even when the **Short-term Reversal** factor is included. This proves the strategy isn't just a "bounce" from yesterday's move, but a reaction to new information.
* **Alpha Focus**: Since the **SMQ** (SI minus QI) portfolio derives its strength from the SI leg, quantitative researchers should prioritize extracting features from **Specific Information** context rather than raw quantifiable data.
* **Scalability**: The survival of the Alpha in the **Liquidity-augmented** model suggests the strategy is not limited to illiquid small-cap stocks.
## Ideas
- `Signal Filtering`: When building NLP sentiment signals, focus your feature extraction on "Specific Information" (textual context, unique events) rather than "Quantifiable Information" (earnings numbers, price-to-book ratios), as the latter is already priced in by standard factors.
- `Factor Orthogonality`: Because SI news survives the "Liquidity" and "Reversal" filters, it is an excellent candidate for a multi-manager portfolio. It provides orthogonal alpha—returns that don't move in sync with the rest of the market.
- `Alpha Decay Analysis`: Since SI news isn't just a "short-term reversal," its signal likely has a longer half-life. You could potentially hold SI-based positions longer than simple momentum-based trades.
