### Explanation (In simple terms)
Imagine you are at an auction where people naturally gravitate toward round numbers like $100 (integers), $100.50 (half-dollars), or $100.05 (nickels). Researchers noticed a massive imbalance of buying just below these numbers and selling just above them. They wanted to know why. Is it because crossing a major line changes how we see the price (like feeling $9.99 is way cheaper than $10.00)? Or is it because traders are trying to "cut the line" by placing orders just a penny before the massive pile of orders sitting at $100? By carefully tracking how stock prices move step-by-step around these numbers, the study proves that traders are mostly trying to undercut the crowd. Traders treat round numbers as major mental anchors for value, creating predictable waves of buying and selling right next to them.

### Key concepts
* **Buy-Sell Imbalance:** An inequality between buying volume and selling volume at specific price points, leading to predictable buying pressure below round numbers and selling pressure above them.
* **Cluster Undercutting Effect:** The behavior where traders place their orders just a tiny bit away from a popular round number (e.g., buying at $9.99 instead of $10.00) to ensure their order gets filled before the massive cluster of orders sitting exactly on the round number.
* **Left-Digit Effect / Threshold Trigger Effect:** Psychological biases where a trader's perception of value shifts dramatically right after a price crosses a threshold because the leftmost digit changes (e.g., transitioning from $9.99 to $10.00).
* **Conditional Analysis:** A rigorous testing method that isolates specific scenarios (such as a price falling *to* an integer versus falling *below* it) and uses control groups (like nickels) to pinpoint the exact cause of a market phenomenon.
* **Reference Points:** Psychological anchors (integers, half-dollars, nickels) that human traders use to simplify valuation, rather than treating the price scale as a perfectly smooth continuum.

### Ideas
* **Psychological Hierarchy of Numbers:** Human cognitive bias directly shapes market liquidity. The stronger the "roundness" of a number (Integers > Half-Dollars > Nickels), the stronger its psychological pull, and the more severe the resulting trading imbalances.
* **Order Book Queue-Jumping:** The dominance of the cluster undercutting effect shows that traders actively game the order book. Sophisticated market participants anticipate where retail or institutional crowds will cluster their orders and strategically place orders just ahead of them to secure execution.
* **Bounded Rationality in Trading:** Stock markets are not purely friction-free or perfectly rational; price action is highly distorted by human cognitive shortcuts (using round numbers as proxies for intrinsic value).
* **Microstructure Strategy Construction:** Traders can build predictive execution models based on these findings. For instance, an algorithm can expect heavy support (buying) just beneath an integer threshold and heavy resistance (selling) just above it, adjusting its limit orders accordingly to optimize execution probability.
