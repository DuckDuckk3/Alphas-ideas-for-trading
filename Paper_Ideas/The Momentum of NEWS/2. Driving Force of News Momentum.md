# Idea 2: Driving Force of News Momentum
## Page 19 - Para 2
In light of the effect of the media in stock prices, what are the asset pricing implications of
news momentum? The answer for this question depends on the `driving force of news momentum`.
If news momentum is driven by `opaque information environment` and/or `media bias`, news
momentum would `induce return momentum` in the `short run`, but stock prices will `finally reverse`
to fundamentals, largely due to the reason that these media shocks are `stationary` and `cannot last
forever` (e.g., Delong, Bradford, Shleifer, Summers, and Waldmann, 1990). This point is
consistent with a large amount of empirical evidence. For example, Tetlock (2007) indicates that
high media pessimism predicts falling downward pressure on market prices followed by a
reversion to fundamentals. In another influential study, Solomon (2012) finds that firms that spin
news by creating more positive media coverage experience subsequent lower stock returns,
though the positive media coverage drives up firms’ stock prices around new announcements. He
attributes this reversal to `investor disappointment` due to the `effects of firms’` past spin. 

## Explaination:
Imagine a company releases news. If that news is presented with a lot of "spin" or hype—or if the market is confused and doesn't have clear information—investors often overreact. They push the stock price up (or down) quickly.
However, because this movement was driven by "noise" (like media bias) rather than actual long-term value, the price eventually snaps back to where it should be. The text is essentially saying: If the media "pumps" a stock, expect a crash later because hype eventually runs out and reality sets in.

## Core Concepts
- News Momentum: The tendency of a stock price to continue moving in the same direction following a news report.
- Asset Pricing Implications: How the cause of the news (the "driving force") determines whether a stock is currently overvalued or undervalued.
- Opaque Information Environment: A situation where it is difficult for investors to get clear, honest facts about a company, making them more reliant on media tone.
- Media Bias/Spin: When news outlets or the companies themselves frame information to sound better (or worse) than it actually is.
- Stationary Shocks: Events or trends that are temporary. Like a wave in the ocean, they go up but must eventually come back down to sea level.
- Price Reversal: The "correction" phase where a stock price returns to its true fundamental value after the initial hype fades.

## Ideas
1. The "Fake News" Correction
The text suggests that media influence acts like a temporary drug for stock prices. While "positive spin" works in the short run to inflate prices, it creates investor disappointment in the long run. When the company fails to live up to the media's hype, investors sell, leading to lower returns later.
2. Media as a Sentiment Mirror
Referencing Tetlock (2007), the text highlights that media "pessimism" can be a self-fulfilling prophecy. If the news is overly negative, it puts downward pressure on prices, but this is often an overreaction. Smart investors look for these moments because they know a reversal to the fundamental value is likely coming.
3. The Danger of "Spin"
Solomon’s (2012) research introduces a warning for companies: manipulating the media might help your stock price today, but you are essentially "borrowing" that growth from the future. Once the "spin" is uncovered, the resulting disappointment causes a price drop that offsets the initial gain.  

**Key Takeaway:** `If the "driving force" behind a stock's movement is just media chatter or a lack of transparency, the price movement is usually a temporary illusion that will eventually reverse.`
